# Rule of Thirds

## Principle

The rule of thirds is a composition technique that divides a visual area into a three-by-three grid using two horizontal and two vertical lines. Placing important elements along these lines or near their intersections can create balance, visual movement, and a more dynamic composition.

In frontend design, this principle can guide the placement of headlines, images, focal points, and other key content. It is a compositional option, not a requirement for every layout.

## When to Use

Apply this principle when designing or reviewing:
- Hero sections with prominent imagery
- Editorial and portfolio layouts
- Image-led landing pages
- Promotional banners and feature sections
- Background images with overlaid content
- Asymmetrical compositions that benefit from visual movement

## How to Apply

- Mentally divide the available composition into a three-by-three grid.
- Consider placing the main subject, image focal point, or headline along a grid line or near an intersection.
- Use the remaining space to accommodate supporting content and preserve breathing room.
- Balance visual weight across the composition rather than placing every element at the center.
- Consider how text, imagery, and calls to action interact within the layout.
- Reevaluate the composition at different viewport sizes, since the effective focal points may change on mobile.
- Use the rule as a starting point, then adjust placement according to the content and intended user experience.

## Do

- Use off-center placement when it creates a clearer or more engaging composition.
- Consider the relationship between the focal point and surrounding whitespace.
- Balance images and text according to their visual weight.
- Ensure important text and actions remain easy to find.
- Combine the rule with visual hierarchy, alignment, and proximity where appropriate.

## Don't

- Force every headline, image, or button onto a grid intersection.
- Assume a centered layout is inherently less effective.
- Leave large empty areas without considering whether they support the composition.
- Sacrifice readability or responsive behavior to preserve the grid.
- Apply the same composition to every page regardless of its purpose.

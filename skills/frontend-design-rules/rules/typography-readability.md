# Typography and Readability

## Principle

Typography should make information easy to read, understand, and navigate. Font choice, letter case, line length, spacing, weight, and hierarchy all influence how users process content.

Design should prioritize communication and usability over decorative treatment. Typography can express personality, but it should not make the message unnecessarily difficult to understand.

## When to Use

Apply this principle when designing or reviewing:
- Headlines and hero sections
- Body text and long-form content
- Navigation labels and buttons
- Forms and input fields
- Captions, badges, and supporting information
- Responsive interfaces with different screen sizes

## How to Apply

- Choose typefaces that suit the product's personality and remain legible at the intended sizes.
- Establish a clear typographic hierarchy through size, weight, spacing, and placement.
- Use sentence case or title case for most interface text where appropriate.
- Reserve all-uppercase text for short labels, acronyms, compact headings, or deliberate stylistic treatments.
- Keep paragraphs and line lengths comfortable to read.
- Use adequate line height and spacing to distinguish separate blocks of content.
- Maintain sufficient contrast between text and its background.
- Test typography at actual desktop and mobile sizes.

## Do

- Make essential information easy to scan.
- Use consistent type scales and text styles.
- Choose font weights that provide distinction without sacrificing legibility.
- Use uppercase selectively, especially for short labels or stylistic emphasis.
- Consider readability across languages, devices, and accessibility settings.
- Let typography support the brand and content rather than overpower them.

## Don't

- Set long paragraphs or entire pages in uppercase without a clear reason.
- Use excessively small text for secondary information that users still need to understand.
- Introduce too many typefaces, sizes, or weights without a purpose.
- Use decorative fonts for content that must be read quickly.
- Sacrifice contrast or legibility for visual novelty.
- Apply fixed word-count rules to determine whether text should be uppercase.

## Exceptions

Display typography, logos, posters, short navigation labels, and expressive editorial layouts may intentionally use uppercase or unconventional type treatments. These choices are appropriate when they suit the context and preserve readability.

Typography requirements also vary by language, typeface, audience, and device. Do not apply a single casing or sizing formula to every interface.

## Validation

- Can users read and understand the content comfortably?
- Is the hierarchy between headings, body text, and supporting information clear?
- Is uppercase used purposefully rather than by default?
- Are line lengths, line height, and spacing appropriate?
- Is text legible across screen sizes and accessibility settings?
- Does the typography reinforce the product's identity without interfering with usability?

The goal is to make content clear and expressive while adapting typographic choices to the needs of each interface.

# Isolation Effect

## Principle

The isolation effect, also known as the Von Restorff effect, describes how an element that differs from its surroundings is more likely to attract attention and be remembered.

In interface design, contrast can help users identify important actions, information, and states. However, differentiation should be intentional. If everything stands out, nothing stands out.

## When to Use

Apply this principle when designing or reviewing:
- Primary calls to action
- Important announcements and promotional content
- Selected navigation items and active states
- Key metrics and dashboard highlights
- Pricing plans and featured options
- Important information within dense layouts

## How to Apply

- Identify the element that genuinely deserves additional attention.
- Differentiate it from surrounding elements using color, size, shape, typography, position, or whitespace.
- Choose a method of contrast that fits the interface's visual identity.
- Keep secondary elements visually quieter when the primary element needs to stand out.
- Use consistent visual treatments for elements that serve the same purpose.
- Ensure that important distinctions remain understandable to users with visual impairments.

## Do

- Use contrast to communicate importance or meaning.
- Make the primary action easy to distinguish from secondary actions.
- Apply emphasis selectively.
- Consider the surrounding elements when evaluating how prominent an item appears.
- Use labels, icons, or other cues alongside color when communicating meaning.

## Don't

- Highlight every button, card, heading, or section equally.
- Add bright colors or unusual shapes purely to attract attention.
- Use visual emphasis to mislead users about the importance of an action.
- Rely on color alone to communicate essential information.
- Assume that the same element must always be the focal point in every context.

## Exceptions

Multiple elements may legitimately need emphasis, such as urgent alerts, competing actions, or data visualizations. In these cases, establish a clear hierarchy of importance rather than forcing every element into a single focal point.

The appropriate treatment also depends on the product, audience, brand identity, and user goals.

## Validation

- Is the highlighted element genuinely important?
- Does it stand out from its surroundings without overwhelming the interface?
- Is the hierarchy between primary and secondary elements clear?
- Does the emphasis help users understand what to do or notice?
- Does the design remain accessible and visually coherent?

The goal is to direct attention intentionally, not to make every interface use the same visual highlight.

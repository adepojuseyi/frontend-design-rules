# Proximity

## Principle

Elements placed close together are generally perceived as related, while elements separated by greater space are more likely to be perceived as distinct. Proximity helps users understand relationships between content without needing additional explanation.

Use spacing to communicate structure and meaning, not just to make an interface look clean.

## When to Use

Apply this principle when designing or reviewing:
- Headlines and supporting text
- Form labels, inputs, and validation messages
- Cards and grouped content
- Navigation items and menus
- Dashboard sections and data groups
- Buttons, descriptions, and related actions

## How to Apply

- Place related elements closer together than unrelated elements.
- Keep headings visually closer to the content they introduce than to the preceding section.
- Group form labels with their corresponding inputs and keep error messages near the fields they describe.
- Use larger spacing to distinguish separate sections or groups.
- Establish consistent spacing relationships across similar components.
- Consider alignment and visual boundaries alongside spacing when relationships are not immediately clear.

## Do

- Use spacing to establish clear content groups.
- Maintain predictable relationships between repeated components.
- Make grouping understandable without relying solely on color.
- Adapt spacing to content density, screen size, and the importance of each section.
- Check that users can quickly identify which labels, descriptions, and actions belong together.

## Don't

- Give every element the same amount of surrounding space regardless of its relationship to other elements.
- Separate a heading from the content it introduces.
- Place unrelated elements so close that users mistake them for a single group.
- Rely on proximity alone when additional labels, borders, or structure are needed for clarity.
- Apply identical spacing everywhere if doing so weakens the hierarchy.

## Exceptions

Dense data tables, compact toolbars, navigation systems, and complex dashboards may require tighter spacing. In these cases, use alignment, dividers, backgrounds, typography, or other cues to preserve clear relationships without wasting space.

## Validation

- Can users tell which elements belong together?
- Are related labels, descriptions, controls, and actions grouped logically?
- Is the spacing between separate sections sufficient to distinguish them?
- Are grouping patterns consistent across similar components?
- Does the spacing improve comprehension rather than simply create empty space?

The goal is to make relationships obvious through intentional spacing, while allowing each interface to use the structure that best fits its content.

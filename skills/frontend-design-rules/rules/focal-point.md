# Focal Point

## Principle

Every interface should guide the viewer's attention toward the most important content. A focal point gives users a natural starting place and helps them understand what matters most.

In left-to-right reading contexts, users often begin scanning near the top-left. However, reading direction, language, layout, device, and established visual patterns all influence where attention begins.

## When to Use

Apply this principle when designing or reviewing:
- Landing page heroes and headlines
- Navigation and page introductions
- Dashboards and data-heavy interfaces
- Product pages and calls to action
- Content layouts with multiple competing elements

## How to Apply

- Identify the single most important message, action, or element for the page.
- Place it where users are likely to notice it naturally, considering the reading direction and layout.
- Use size, contrast, typography, position, whitespace, and visual weight to establish prominence.
- Create a clear path from the focal point to supporting content.
- Ensure the focal point reflects the user's goal, not merely the designer's preference.
- On responsive layouts, reconsider placement and hierarchy for smaller screens rather than assuming desktop positioning will work.

## Do

- Make the page's purpose understandable at a glance.
- Give important content enough space to stand out.
- Ensure the focal point leads naturally to relevant information or actions.
- Adapt placement to the interface, audience, and reading patterns.

## Don't

- Force every interface to place its main element in the top-left corner.
- Make multiple unrelated elements compete equally for attention.
- Use size or color for emphasis without considering meaning.
- Assume the largest element is automatically the most important.
- Sacrifice usability or accessibility for a dramatic composition.

## Exceptions

A focal point does not always need to appear in the top-left. Centered hero sections, full-screen imagery, editorial layouts, maps, dashboards, and immersive experiences may benefit from different entry points. Choose the placement that best supports the content and intended user journey.

## Validation

- Can users quickly identify the page's main purpose?
- Is the most important element visually distinguishable from supporting content?
- Does attention flow naturally through the interface?
- Does the focal point remain clear on mobile and desktop?
- Does the composition serve the audience and product rather than follow a fixed formula?

The goal is to guide attention intentionally, not to make every interface look the same.

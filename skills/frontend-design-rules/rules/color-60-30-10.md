# 60–30–10 Color Rule

## Principle

Use three levels of color emphasis to create visual balance, reinforce brand identity, and guide attention.

The 60–30–10 rule is a starting point for distributing color across an interface, not a strict measurement requirement.

## When to Use

Apply this rule when choosing or reviewing colors for:

- Landing pages
- Hero sections
- Dashboards
- Cards and content sections
- Marketing pages
- UI components and calls to action

## How to Apply

Use three color roles:

- **60% — Dominant color:** The primary background or largest surface area. It establishes the overall visual atmosphere.
- **30% — Secondary color:** A complementary or contrasting color used for supporting surfaces, content regions, and secondary visual elements.
- **10% — Accent color:** A brand or emphasis color used selectively to draw attention to important actions, highlights, links, or selected states.

Treat these percentages as approximate visual proportions, not exact pixel measurements.

## Do

- Assign clear roles to the dominant, secondary, and accent colors.
- Use the accent color selectively so it retains its impact.
- Check that text and interactive elements have sufficient contrast against their backgrounds.
- Reuse existing brand colors and design tokens when available.
- Consider the overall visual weight of images, gradients, and large colored surfaces.

## Don't

- Distribute accent colors everywhere.
- Introduce new colors unnecessarily when a project already has an established palette.
- Sacrifice readability or accessibility to preserve the ratio.
- Assume that every component or individual section must follow the same percentages.

## Exceptions

The ratio can vary depending on the interface, brand, content, and purpose.

Data visualizations, status indicators, image-heavy layouts, dark themes, and accessibility requirements may need different color distributions.

Do not force the ratio when doing so would reduce clarity or usability.

## Validation

- Is there a clear dominant color?
- Does the secondary color support rather than compete with it?
- Is the accent color used selectively?
- Do important actions stand out?
- Is text readable and are interactive states distinguishable?

If the colors compete for attention, simplify the palette and clarify the role of each color.
